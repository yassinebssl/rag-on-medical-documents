\paragraph{Why the gates let them through.} The highest scores go to questions whose passages come from the right document: N06 retrieves the WHO tuberculosis and comorbidities module, N08 the WHO antenatal care recommendations, N10 a review of pulmonary arterial hypertension treatment. The passages are on the exact topic, so both gates rate them high although the asked value is not there. A gate that scores topical relevance cannot tell ``this passage is about the question'' from ``this passage answers it''. The cross-encoder also passed N02 and N04, which the cosine gate stopped, and N09 came close (0.437).

\paragraph{What caught them.} The model's own refusal, as the prompt instructs. It held every time here, but it is the least controlled defence: another model may answer from memory and cite a passage that is merely on topic, which the citation check would accept.

\paragraph{A check on the labels.} When passages score as high as for N06 (0.977) and N08 (0.948), the fact could be in the corpus after all, and the refusal would then be a missed answer. We searched the text of all 3,410 chunks for the asked values. The 12 chunks that mention the tuberculin test give no size in millimetres (N06); none of the 49 chunks on pre-eclampsia mentions aspirin (N08); the 13 chunks that mention sotatercept, from two reviews, give no dose (N10); and no chunk mentions thyroid cancer (N02) or the REDUCE-IT trial (N04). A keyword search can miss a paraphrase, but it found nothing against the five refusals.

\subsection{A refusal the passages justify, hidden by the labels (A19)}

A19 asks for the starting dose of basal insulin added to a GLP-1 receptor agonist. On the first day, the model spent the whole 600-token budget on hidden reasoning for \code{rag_rerank} and returned no text; the same request, sent again for \code{rag_hybrid}, came back as the refusal sentence. \code{call_llm} now resends once with 4,000 tokens, and temperature 0 did not make the hosted model deterministic. Asked again on 2026-11-16, the model refused for both systems. The refusal is right for the passages it was given: all five come from the expected article, but none holds the evidence sentence (``starting at 10 units/day or 0.1--0.2 units/kg/day''). The chunk that holds it ranks 8th in both dense retrieval and BM25, and 7th after fusion, just outside the five. Our labels hide this miss: a PubMed article has no page, so every passage of the right article counts as relevant and as a page hit. The fault is in retrieval, not in the model, and only a passage-level label would show it. The \code{no_rag} baseline answered A19 from memory with the values of the evidence sentence: here the retrieval miss cost an answer that the model alone gave.

\subsection{Confident baseline answers from other guidelines}

The \code{no_rag} baseline answered 37 of the 40 questions. We read its 19 answers to answerable questions against the expected answers. Six state another value than the corpus:
\begin{itemize}
\item A02 (stage~2 hypertension). Expected, from a WHO guideline: 160/100~mmHg and above. Baseline: 140/90~mmHg, from the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline, with a note that other guidelines use 160/100.
\item A03 (maximum daily sodium). Expected, from the WHO sodium guideline: less than 2~g. Baseline: less than 2,300~mg, attributed to several bodies, WHO among them.
\item A05 (LDL cholesterol goal in diabetes). Expected: below 100~mg/dL. Baseline: below 70~mg/dL, or below 55 with cardiovascular disease, from ADA and ESC/EAS.
\item A09 (start of antiretroviral therapy during tuberculosis treatment). Expected: within two weeks, whatever the CD4 count. Baseline, attributed to WHO: within 2 weeks below 50 cells/mm$^3$, within 8 weeks otherwise.
\item A14 (maximum paracetamol dose for older people under 50~kg). Expected: 60~mg/kg a day. Baseline: 3~g a day; at 40~kg, 60~mg/kg is 2.4~g.
\item A18 (SGLT2 inhibitors under NICE). Expected: preferred first-line therapy, with metformin. Baseline: metformin first, SGLT2 inhibitors first-line only in high-risk groups.
\end{itemize}
A01 gets no single value (the answer lists the thresholds of several guidelines) and A17 is refused. The other twelve contain the expected fact, sometimes among other values. The six answers follow real guidelines of other bodies or years, which is the danger: the answer cannot be traced, mixes guidelines without saying which applies, and nothing flags it. The RAG systems gave the corpus's values, with their source. \subsection{Retrieval misses that did not hurt the answer}

For A06 (parenteral artesunate) and A07 (primaquine), the hybrid passages miss the evidence page (pages 14 and 12 of the WHO malaria guideline), yet the answers state the expected facts, cited to other pages of the same guideline (75 to 86, and 35 to 46). Our labels are coarse both ways: the page hit rate counts these as misses, while hit rate and context precision count any passage from the right document as relevant.

Fusion explains both misses (\code{notebooks/02_retrieval_and_refusal_gate.ipynb}). For A06, dense retrieval ranks page 14 fifth but BM25 does not return it, so its single fusion term, $1/(60+5)$, is passed by chunks that both lists return, and it ends 14th. For A07, BM25 ranks page 12 fourth, dense retrieval 16th, and it ends 7th. Fusion rewards what both rankings agree on, so a page found by one alone can drop out. Here page 79 repeats the recommendation (artesunate for at least 24\,h, then an ACT); a recommendation stated once would be lost.

\subsection{Passages from another document}

For A04 (starting dose of metformin), the first passage comes from the article on SGLT2 inhibitors and the WHO passage with the answer is second (context precision 0.5); the answer cites only the WHO passage. For A02, the answer cites the expected WHO guideline and, between two such citations, a passage from a WHO diabetes guideline. The label counts that passage as not relevant, the citation check accepts its number, and the judge counted the claim as supported; no automatic check says whether that passage states the definition.

\subsection{Citations: location, authority and date}

A PubMed article is cited by its title only, since its text has no pages: the answer to A14 cites the same title four times in a row, and a reader must search a whole article (27,564 bytes of text) for the sentence. A13, the brief's own example (adult dose of paracetamol), is answered from a study of dosing errors reported to a poisons centre, since no WHO guideline of the corpus states it; a reader may take the article for a guideline. A18 asks about ``the latest NICE guidelines''; the answer is only as recent as the article in the corpus.
